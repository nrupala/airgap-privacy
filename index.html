Privacy Policy — AirGap Keyboard

Effective for AirGap Keyboard version 2.6.1 and later.

The short version

AirGap Keyboard cannot send your data anywhere. The app does not have the INTERNET permission — the permission Android apps need before they can open any network connection — so there is no channel by which anything you type, say, copy, or store in the keyboard could reach us or anyone else. We collect nothing, because the app is built so that collection is impossible, not merely switched off.

You do not have to take our word for this. See “How you can verify every claim in this policy” below.

What the app does with your information

Typing. Everything you type passes through the keyboard into the app you are typing in — that is what a keyboard is. The keyboard itself keeps only what it needs to help you type: words it learns from your typing and words you save deliberately. Those live in a single storage file on your device, encrypted with AES-256-GCM, with the encryption key generated and held by the Android Keystore on your phone. The key never leaves your phone, and we never see it.

Clipboard. Clipboard history and pinned clips the keyboard keeps for you are stored in the same encrypted file. Unpinned clips expire automatically after one hour. The keyboard's clipboard is switched off in password fields.

Voice. When you dictate, your speech is transcribed on your phone by a speech model stored on the device. Your audio exists only in your phone's memory during the dictation; the audio buffer is wiped immediately after transcription. Audio is never saved to storage and never leaves the device. The microphone is used only while you are dictating, and it stops when you pause, change fields, or put the keyboard away.

Password and private fields. In password, incognito, and no-personalized-learning fields, the keyboard turns off suggestions, voice, clipboard, and learning. Nothing typed in those fields is read into the keyboard's storage, and nothing from storage is offered there.

Scheduled messages and auto-send. A message you schedule is held in the same encrypted storage on your device. At its scheduled time you can send it with one tap. The app also offers an optional auto-send service: it does nothing unless you personally switch on “AirGap auto-send” in Android's Accessibility settings. Its only job is to deliver a message you composed and scheduled, into the conversation you wrote it in, after checking that the app and conversation match and the message field is empty. If anything does not match, it sends nothing and tells you why. It reads nothing else and serves no other purpose.

Backups. Android backup is switched off for this app. The keyboard's stored data is not included in device backups or cloud restores.

Purchases. The app offers one optional purchase: a one-time unlock of the premium voice features beyond their free daily allowance. The purchase is handled entirely by Google Play in the Play Store app. The keyboard receives only the result — unlocked or not — and never sees your payment details, your Google account, or any purchase information beyond that entitlement. Typing, the encrypted clipboard, and the secure-field behaviour are free and are never part of any purchase.

What leaves the app — and only by your hand

The keyboard itself never transmits anything. A few doors open only when you use them, and each one warns you at the moment you use it:

  • Exporting your dictionary saves a file where you choose to put it. From that moment the file is in your hands, not ours.
  • Sharing a selection or inserting a GIF places that content into the other app you are typing in, under that app's own privacy practices.
  • Getting a speech model: the app never downloads anything. Settings lists a catalogue of verified speech models. If you tap “Get” for one, your browser fetches the file from the supplier; you then import it, and the keyboard checks the file's SHA-256 fingerprint against the catalogue before trusting it — a file that is not in the catalogue is refused. Your browser's activity is governed by your browser, not by this policy.
  • Feature requests: if you choose to send one, the app first tells you that you are leaving for your browser, and only proceeds if you agree.

What we collect

Nothing. There is no AirGap account, no sign-up, no analytics, no telemetry, no advertising, and no crash reporting in the app. We do not receive your typed text, your voice, your clipboard contents, your learned words, or information about your device or how you use the app.

The app contains no ads. Its one optional purchase is described under “Purchases” above and is handled by Google Play, not by us.

Children

AirGap Keyboard is intended for users aged 18 and over and is not directed at children.

How you can verify every claim in this policy

  • Permissions: Android Settings → Apps → AirGap Keyboard → Permissions shows exactly two permissions: Microphone, and Google Play Billing (the channel to the Play Store app for the optional unlock — it is not a network permission). No INTERNET permission exists for this app.
  • In the app: Settings → About states each quality claim, how it is enforced, and how you can check it. Settings → About → Proof runs live checks on your phone — reading the installed app's permissions back from Android, attempting a network connection Android refuses, and running encryption round-trip and tamper tests with your phone's own key.
  • Airplane mode test: turn on airplane mode and dictate. It still works — because nothing about the app needs a network.

Changes to this policy

If a future version changes any of this, the updated policy will be published at the same address before that version is released, and the claims in the app's About screen will be updated to match. The no-network construction is the product's foundation: a version that requested the INTERNET permission would be a different product, and its permission list would show it.
